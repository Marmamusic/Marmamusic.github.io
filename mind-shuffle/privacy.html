Mind Shuffle privacy

Android editions by marma · Updated 29 October 2026

Contact: marma.developer@gmail.com.

Your game and audio

Progress, your unfinished round, selected level and mute preference stay on your device. The game has no player account, cloud progress service, microphone access or developer-operated analytics. Music and tap sounds play locally. Muting silences both. Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling removes local progress.

Full edition

The Full Android edition has no advertising SDK and requests no Android permissions. The game works offline. If you buy it through Google Play, Google handles the payment; this app does not receive payment-card details.

Free edition advertising

The Free Android edition includes Google Mobile Ads and Google's User Messaging Platform. Google's SDKs may process IP addresses, device identifiers, ad interactions and diagnostic information for advertising, measurement and fraud prevention. Ads need a network connection; the memory game itself works offline.

The app refreshes consent information at launch and waits until Google's consent system permits ad requests before initializing advertising. Required consent forms appear through Google's tools. When available, the native Ad privacy button lets you reopen advertising privacy choices. Ads appear outside rounds, never over the board.

See Google's Privacy Policy and the Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure for Google's processing and retention information. The browser and standalone HTML previews do not include the native advertising SDK.

Your choices

You can mute audio with the speaker button, manage available ad privacy choices, or erase local game data through Android's app settings. Removing local data does not cancel a purchase recorded by Google Play. Google controls retention of data processed through its services.

Audience and distribution

The game offers memory puzzles without accounts, chat or user uploads. Its final store audience and advertising settings are declared by the publisher in Google Play and AdMob. The privately hosted web preview uses its hosting provider's access controls, separate from the offline Android app.
